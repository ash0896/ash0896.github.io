About Me

Computational & bioinorganic chemist bridging drug design, quantum chemistry, and machine learning.

I'm a computational and bioinorganic chemist with 4+ years of research experience spanning structure-based drug design, molecular dynamics/docking of membrane-protein targets, and quantum chemical modelling of metalloenzyme reactivity — with projects ranging from A3 adenosine GPCR ligand recognition to copper-dependent lytic polysaccharide monooxygenases (LPMOs) and synthetic diiron peroxo complexes.

I currently serve as the lead computational chemist within the multi-institutional ERC Synergy Grant CUBE consortium (Germany/Norway/Italy), based at the Max Planck Institute for Chemical Energy Conversion in the group of Prof. Dr. Serena DeBeer. My work combines DFT/QM-MM calculations with experimental X-ray spectroscopy and kinetics data, automated through reproducible Python/HPC pipelines.

Beyond the bench and the terminal, I enjoy mentoring junior researchers, training students in spectroscopy practicals, and contributing to the scientific community as an elected PhD representative and conference co-organizer. I'm also enthusiastic about learning and applying quantum computing approaches to drug discovery problems.
